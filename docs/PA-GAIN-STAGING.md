# Gain staging: XR18 into the JBL PRX ONE and Fender Passport

The goal: **all mixing happens on the XR18** (where Mix Assistant works), and the speakers are set once and left alone. Most hiss in small systems comes from the opposite: a quiet signal leaving the mixer, turned up a lot at the speaker.

You'll need: the X Air app, and a free sound-level meter app on a phone (any "SPL meter" app is close enough).

## 1. Make the speakers plain amplifiers

**PRX ONE** (it has its own 7-channel mixer):
- Use only the inputs the XR18 feeds. Turn every other channel all the way down.
- Effects (reverb, chorus, delay) off on those channels. The XR18 does effects.
- EQ flat on those channels.
- Bluetooth off during services, so nobody's phone can play through it.

**Passport** (also has a built-in mixer):
- Same idea: only the channels fed by the XR18 up, others down; its reverb off; EQ flat.

## 2. Mono or stereo?

If there's **one PRX ONE** for the room, anything panned left or right on the XR18 (like Keys L/R) loses half its sound if only Main L is plugged in. Either:
- plug **XR18 Main L and Main R** into **two PRX ONE inputs** (it adds them together), or
- keep the XR18 channels panned to the centre.

## 3. Set the XR18 side first

1. Each channel's **gain** so normal singing or speaking peaks around **-18 to -12** on its meter (see the earlier gain-staging notes for the Shure and Phenyx Pro).
2. Channel faders around **0** for the main voices.
3. **Main L/R fader at 0 dB.** In a normal song, the main meter should peak around **-12 to -6**. It should never hit red.

## 4. Then set the speakers to match

With the band and a singer running at normal service level:
1. Start the speaker's input gain and master volume **low**.
2. Bring the speaker's **input gain** up until its input meter or signal light shows strong signal but **never clip/red**. A line-level signal from the XR18 needs much less gain than a microphone.
3. Bring the speaker's **master volume** up until the room is at the right level with the phone meter in the middle of the room:
   - Speaking: about **75–80 dBA**
   - Worship: about **85–90 dBA** at the loudest
4. Mark these settings with tape or a photo. **Don't touch the speakers during the service.** Any adjustment is made on the XR18 (or by Mix Assistant).

## 5. If the Passport is a monitor or second speaker

If the Passport is used as a stage monitor, a pastor's wedge or a fill for the back of the room, feed it from an **XR18 aux bus**, not the main mix:
- Set that aux bus's master at 0 dB and set the Passport's level the same way as above.
- **Name that bus in the X Air app** (e.g. "Pastor Wedge" or "Back Fill"). Mix Assistant then keeps the click and guide off it automatically.

## 6. Check for hiss

With nobody making sound and the room quiet, stand in front of each speaker:
- Hiss mostly gone when you pull the XR18 main fader down? The hiss is coming from the mixer channels: mute unused channels (Mix Assistant does this) and check channel gains.
- Hiss still there with the XR18 main fader down? It's the speaker's own gain: lower its input gain and raise the XR18 output instead.

## What I still need to know

So this can be made exact for your setup:
- Which XR18 outputs go to the PRX ONE and to the Passport, and into which of their inputs?
- What does each speaker do: main room speaker, monitor, pastor's wedge, lobby, back fill?
- Which Passport model is it (Event, Venue, Conference, or an older 150/300/500)?
